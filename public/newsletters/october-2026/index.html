It’s been a busy few months, with multiple projects across Europe and the team doing rather well on Avios points…

In September, The Superyacht Report ran an article of ours across eight pages of its Owners Focus issue, entitled The Unsecured Owner. This is an often-overlooked topic that comes up on the owner’s side most weeks: where the buyer’s money sits if a shipyard becomes insolvent.

The article covers the protection of stage payments, practical limits of refund guarantees in yachting, who owns a hull whilst it is still in the shed, and what happens when it goes wrong. Much of the article draws on our own shipyard suitability research and the financial due diligence that we often carry out for new build clients, including current stories like The Italian Sea Group proceedings in Florence this year - which have yet to reach a conclusion.

Most yachts are, of course, delivered as contracted, and most yards are presently enjoying the fruits of a healthy order book. Owners are in the strongest position when the yard’s finances are understood from the outset, the contract and payment schedule are drawn to suit, and independent advice is taken before the first instalment is paid. The issue is free to read in full on Issuu, and a companion piece on our website covers the same ground with the shipyard data in a table.

SeaTime Tracker

SeaTime Tracker, our app for logging sea time from a vessel’s AIS position, has expanded this autumn into the commercial workboat industry. Recent releases add a shift clock-in for crew transfer vessels and other shift work, an on and off board setting for rotational crew, night watch hours, and mileage from the AIS track. Sea days are counted to each authority’s own rules, so crews working towards US Coast Guard, AMSA or Maritime New Zealand certificates are covered, as well as the MCA.

For anyone in our network working towards a certificate, yacht crew or otherwise, details are at forelandmarine.com/tools/seatime-tracker.

Tipner West

The Tipner West site, Portsmouth. Imagery © 2022 Google

In Portsmouth, work continues on Foreland Shipyard Group’s proposal for a superyacht refit yard at Tipner West. The site sits within the city’s Local Plan, currently under examination by the Planning Inspectorate, with the Tipner West policy heard on Wednesday 21 October. A 30,000m² site with three 50m sheds and a 500-tonne travel hoist is at the centre of the plans, with around 150 people on site at full operation. As part of our heritage pledge, the site’s listed Georgian powder magazines would be revitalised and brought back into use as offices, crew areas and warehousing.

Over the summer we have met with Amanda Martin, MP for Portsmouth North, and with the Leader and Chief Executive of Portsmouth City Council, both of whom have expressed great interest in the development. Our meeting with Cllr Steve Pitt centred on jobs and apprenticeships for the city, and on the further opportunity for local businesses supplying a working yard. Discussions with the Council on how the facility is funded are ongoing; it may be privately funded or partially supported by the local authority.

One of the pressure points for UK shipbuilding and refit is the current tax arrangement: foreign-flagged, privately owned yachts refitting in the UK are subject to VAT, whereas in Spain, for example, these works are zero-rated. An extension of the Solent Freeport to the Tipner site would help locally, but the United Kingdom as a whole needs a tax regime similar to the EU Inward Processing Relief (TPA) to stimulate trade in the sector, and there is considerable appetite for one across the industry. We will be working with industry colleagues to present a proposal to the Treasury Committee next year. More on this in the next edition.

The First Owner’s Reference

The First Owner’s Reference 1st Edition goes to print in January 2027. The web edition is open at firstownersreference.com, and comments from clients and partners are welcome before copy closes, at editors@firstownersreference.com.
