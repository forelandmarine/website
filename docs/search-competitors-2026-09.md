# Search competitors for forelandmarine.com

Compiled 9 September 2026 from thirteen live SERPs across Foreland's commercial and informational target set. Positions are as returned on the day; treat them as a snapshot, not a ranking report.

Foreland appears on eight of the eleven core SERPs, with a best position of 1. That is a strong base. The interesting part is the three it misses and the one term where it competes against itself.

## Tier 1: direct rivals, same positioning

Independent, owner-side, new build and refit project management. These are the firms a prospect is choosing between when they choose Foreland.

**ULTIMAR** (ultimar.yachts) is the closest analogue in the market and the most consistent presence alongside Foreland on owner's-representation queries, taking three or four results on each. SYBAss and IAMI certified, YORR listed, founded 2023. Jack Inglis is a contributor to The First Owner's Reference, so this is a competitor and a correspondent at once.

**superyachtnewbuild.com** takes position 1 on the head owner's-representative query, ahead of Foreland. Twenty-four years, positioned purely on new build supervision.

**Winterbothams** (winterbothams.co.uk) appears on two SERPs at position 3, UK-based, pairing independent marine surveying with project management. Closest to Foreland on geography and framing.

**ACP Surveyors**, **7Ps Yacht Support**, **The Yacht Consultant**, **Praxis Marine**, **ABL Yachts**, **NauticALL**, **Yacht Consultancy** and **BSM Yachting** form the long tail. Each takes one SERP. None appears across the set the way Foreland and ULTIMAR do, which suggests a fragmented field where consistency of coverage is itself a differentiator.

## Tier 2: multi-line management houses

Larger, older, and structurally not independent, which is the argument Foreland makes against them. They still outrank it.

**Hill Robinson** takes position 1 for refit project management. It runs management, brokerage and in-house technical together, which is precisely the conflated model the publication writes about, and it is beating Foreland on Foreland's own service term.

**Ocean Independence** is the strongest generalist in the data: three SERPs, best position 1, present on both service and cost queries. Brokerage and management combined.

**Fraser**, **Camper & Nicholsons**, **Wilson Yacht Management**, **JMS Yachting**, **Luxury Yacht Group** and **Fairport Global** fill the rest. Fraser and Ocean Independence both hold top-three positions for the exact phrase "yacht refit project management".

## Tier 3: answer competitors

Not service rivals, but they take the informational SERPs that feed the funnel, and they are the ones to beat on AEO.

**yachtcostcalculator.com** is the direct threat to both running-cost calculators. It appears on two cost queries and is built purely as calculator-plus-guide content.

**YachtBuyer** holds position 1 on the 10 percent rule query and also runs a project-managers directory, so it competes on both sides.

**Moran Yachts**, **Edmiston**, **SuperYachtReview**, **Rightboat**, **Yachttrading**, **Ocean Independence** and a cluster of small brokerages publish cost-guide content that consistently outranks specialist firms on those queries. **Boat International** is the trade-press presence.

On the crew salary query the competition is entirely recruitment: **Yotspot**, **Flying Fish**, **Talent Gurus**, **Yachtly Crew**, **Bespoke Crew**. Foreland sits seventh among them.

## Tier 4: technical and naval architecture

A segment Foreland claims in its service copy and does not rank in at all.

**Owen Clarke Design** dominates it, taking three of nine results on the performance sailing refit query and two of nine on technical consultancy. **Lateral Engineering**, **Brookes Bell**, **BCTQ**, **AMC Marine**, **H+B technics** and **Design Asset Management** hold the rest.

## Where Foreland is absent

Three SERPs returned no Foreland result at all.

**Superyacht refit project management, as a service term.** Hill Robinson, ACP Surveyors, Winterbothams, 7Ps, The Yacht Consultant and Wilson Yacht Management take it. This is a core commercial term and the page exists.

**Performance sailing yacht refit, rig and keel.** Owen Clarke Design, VGYD and The Rig Shop take it. This is Foreland's stated specialism and there is a 3,000-word article on it that is not ranking.

**Yacht technical consultancy and naval architecture.** Owen Clarke, Lateral, Brookes Bell and BCTQ take it.

## The sharpest finding: /refit is competing with its own article

On the exact phrase "yacht refit project management", the page that appears for Foreland is `/insights/refit-project-management-what-to-expect`, at position 8. The service page `/refit`, whose title is literally "Yacht Refit Project Management", does not appear anywhere.

The article and the service page are competing for the same term and Google has picked the article, which is the weaker page for a commercial query because it does not convert. Fraser, Ocean Independence, Hill Robinson and Monaco Marine hold the positions above.

This is worth fixing before any new content is commissioned, and it does not require a new URL, so it sits inside the standing constraints. The likely causes, in order of probability: `/refit` is 1,240 words against competitors running two to four times that; the article carries the more specific title and more internal links; and there is no clear hierarchy telling Google which of the two is the canonical answer for the term. The remedy is to make `/refit` substantially the deeper page, point the article at it as the commercial destination rather than treating them as peers, and differentiate the article's title toward the "what to expect" long tail it is actually serving.

The same pattern probably explains the performance sailing absence: `/insights/performance-sailing-yacht-refit` is 3,031 words and ranks for nothing, while Owen Clarke holds the segment with design-authority pages. That one is a harder fight, because Owen Clarke are naval architects publishing original design work, and the honest question is whether Foreland wants to contest a design-led SERP or target the operator-side variants instead.

## What this does not tell you

Search position is not market share. This is eleven queries on one day, US-weighted, with no volume data behind it. Nothing here is a substitute for Search Console, which would show what Foreland actually gets impressions and clicks for rather than what a sample of SERPs suggests. That remains the largest missing input.
