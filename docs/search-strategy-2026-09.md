# Search strategy: how Foreland beats the field

Written 9 September 2026, against the competitor set recorded in `search-competitors-2026-09.md`. This is a plan for the next four quarters, not a task list. The technical work is finished; what follows is about position.

## Where the position actually is

Foreland appears on eight of eleven core SERPs with a best position of one, which is a stronger base than the May strategy assumed. The field it competes in is not one field but four, and they need different answers.

The independent project management firms are peers. Foreland matches or beats most of them and is beaten by ULTIMAR and superyachtnewbuild.com on the owner's-representation terms. The multi-line management houses, Hill Robinson and Ocean Independence in particular, hold the top of the service queries on domain authority Foreland cannot match in a year. The cost and salary queries are held by brokerages and recruiters publishing undated, unsourced content. And the technical and naval architecture queries belong to Owen Clarke Design and the engineering consultancies, where Foreland does not appear at all.

Two of those four are winnable in twelve months. One is winnable on a specific flank. The fourth should be conceded.

## The structural advantage, and why it holds

Hill Robinson runs management, brokerage and in-house technical together. Ocean Independence runs brokerage, charter and management. Fraser, Camper & Nicholsons and Moran are brokerages with management arms. Every one of them outranks Foreland on at least one commercial term, and not one of them can publish an honest account of how retrocessions work, why a broker should not choose the owner's manager, or what a captain's introduction fee does to a yard recommendation. Publishing it would indict the model that pays for the website.

That is the whole strategy in one paragraph. Foreland's content advantage is not effort or frequency, both of which a larger firm can outspend. It is the set of true and useful things a conflicted competitor is structurally unable to say. Those subjects are also, not coincidentally, the ones a first-time buyer searches for at the point of highest anxiety and lowest knowledge.

The advantage is already working where it has been used. The publication holds page one for the 10 percent rule and for refit cost, and its framing is being reproduced almost verbatim in generated answers, because it is the only source in the result set with a named author, a dated figure and a declared interest. It is not working on the service pages, which until this week read like everyone else's service pages.

The rule that follows: every page Foreland publishes should answer a question a conflicted competitor would have to fudge. Where a page cannot pass that test, it is a page the larger firms will win on authority.

## Tier one, the independent firms

ULTIMAR, superyachtnewbuild.com, Winterbothams, ACP Surveyors, 7Ps, The Yacht Consultant, Praxis Marine, ABL Yachts, NauticALL, BSM Yachting.

These firms are the real commercial competition and the easiest to out-rank, because the field is fragmented. Only ULTIMAR appears across multiple SERPs alongside Foreland; the rest hold one query each. None publishes at the depth Foreland now does on refit, and none has a publication behind it.

The play is coverage consistency rather than any single page. Foreland has thirty-three articles and four service pages against competitors running eight to fifteen pages of thin service copy. That advantage compounds if the service pages are treated as the destination and the articles as the supporting cluster, and it dissipates if the two compete, which is exactly what was happening on refit until this week.

Concretely: apply the refit treatment to the other three service pages. `/owners-representation` currently ranks third with two articles behind it at four and five, which is the correct hierarchy and needs only depth. `/yacht-management` ranks second behind Ocean Independence and needs the same. `/technical-consultancy` does not rank at all and is the weakest of the four. Each should carry what the refit page now carries: real numbers, the mechanics of how the work is priced, the trade-off the owner is actually choosing between, and a fee discussion. Competitors publish none of that because publishing a fee invites a comparison they would rather not have.

One housekeeping item. `/insights/the-role-of-an-owners-representative` and `/insights/what-is-a-yacht-owners-representative` are close enough in subject that they are likely splitting the same signal. They should be differentiated or merged.

## Tier two, the multi-line houses

Hill Robinson, Ocean Independence, Fraser, Camper & Nicholsons, Wilson Yacht Management, JMS, Luxury Yacht Group, Moran.

Do not contest these head-on. Hill Robinson holds position one for refit project management on twenty years of domain history and a client list Foreland cannot yet match. Trying to outrank it for "superyacht refit" as a bare term is a multi-year fight for a query whose intent is diffuse.

Contest the qualified variants instead, where the qualifier is the thing the competitor cannot claim. "Independent refit project management", "owner-side project management", "refit project manager not affiliated with the yard", "yacht manager independent of broker". These have lower volume and far higher intent, and the multi-line firms are structurally disqualified from competing for them without inviting the obvious question.

Then take the comparison queries directly. An owner searching "owner's representative vs yacht manager" or "should my broker recommend my yacht manager" is at the exact decision Foreland exists to serve, and the honest answer favours Foreland. Foreland already has the first of those. The second does not exist yet and should.

The publication does this work at one remove and does it better, because a reader trusts an editorial source over a service provider making the same argument. Chapter three is already the strongest asset in the estate for this purpose.

## Tier three, the answer competitors

yachtcostcalculator.com, YachtBuyer, Moran, Edmiston, SuperYachtReview, Rightboat, Yachttrading, and the crew recruiters on the salary queries.

These are the AEO battleground and the most winnable of the four, because the incumbent content is weak in a specific and exploitable way. It is undated, unsourced, generically written, and mostly published by parties with a transaction interest in the answer. A language model choosing between an undated brokerage page asserting ten percent and a dated, sourced, named-author page explaining why the figure is twelve to fifteen will increasingly choose the second. That is already happening on two queries.

The requirements are mechanical and the estate now meets most of them: a named author, a visible date, a source for every figure, a declared interest, and structured data that carries the definition rather than just the label. The remaining work is coverage. The publication has four reference pages; it should have eight to ten by the second edition, each owning a query where the incumbent answer is a brokerage guess.

The highest-value additions, in order of the gap between query value and incumbent quality: yacht insurance cost and what drives it, where the incumbents are insurance brokers; berth and marina cost by region, where there is almost no honest published data; crew rotation economics, where the recruiters publish salary but not the true cost of rotation; and depreciation by segment and age, which nobody publishes because brokers have no interest in publishing it.

## Tier four, the technical consultancies

Owen Clarke Design, Lateral Engineering, Brookes Bell, BCTQ, AMC Marine, Design Asset Management.

Concede the design-led queries. Owen Clarke are naval architects publishing original design work on rig, keel and appendage optimisation, and they have earned that ground. Foreland is not a design house and should not pretend to be one on a SERP where the comparison is unflattering.

There is a real flank, though. Owen Clarke sell design; Foreland sells the management of the work that follows from a design. The operator-side variants are open: commissioning a rig, managing a keel modification through class approval, running a performance programme across a season, specifying and tendering appendage work. Those are project-management queries wearing technical clothing, and the design houses do not compete for them because they do not do the work.

`/technical-consultancy` should be rebuilt on that basis. It currently reads as a list of six disciplines, which is a capability statement rather than an answer to anything, and it ranks for nothing.

## The two-domain rule

The estate now has two properties competing in the same result sets, and the calculator showed what happens without governance: identical headings, identical FAQ questions and three different figures for the same thing.

The rule is that the publication owns the question and Foreland owns the engagement. Where a query is informational, the publication answers it and Foreland links to it. Where a query implies hiring somebody, Foreland answers it and the publication cites Foreland's page as the practitioner reference. Neither should publish a second version of what the other already covers, and where both must cover the same ground, they cover it at different depths and say so.

One figure per subject across both domains. Where a number is revised, it is revised in both places on the same day. Chapter one's basis is the house position on running cost, and everything else conforms to it.

## Off-site, which is still the constraint

Nothing on-page substitutes for the fact that Hill Robinson and Ocean Independence have fifteen years of accumulated citation and Foreland has two. The list has not changed since May and remains the highest-value unexecuted work in the plan.

The legal thought-leadership route is the most realistic within six to nine months, because the firms concerned publish constantly and need practitioner input for the operational half of what they write. Stephenson Harwood, HFW, Hill Dickinson, Harneys and Conyers are the targets, and the flag-state and ownership-structure material is the natural hook.

Knight Frank and Capgemini sit on a twelve to eighteen month horizon and are worth pursuing because the publication already cites both, which makes the approach a conversation rather than a cold pitch. Spear's, Citywealth and Campden Wealth run in parallel and reach the adviser layer around the buyer rather than the buyer.

The trade press remains available and underused. One bylined piece per quarter in Boat International, SuperyachtNews, Yachting World, Seahorse or The Superyacht Report, on a subject the publication has already researched, converts existing work into a citation at low marginal cost.

## Sequence

The first quarter is the three remaining service pages, treated as `/refit` has been, and the two owner's-representative articles differentiated. This is the work with the shortest path between effort and ranking, because the pages exist and the competition is thin.

The second quarter is the reference-page programme on the publication, four new pages against the weakest incumbent answers, and the first two legal thought-leadership placements.

The third quarter is the technical consultancy rebuild on the operator-side flank, and the Knight Frank and Spear's approaches.

Throughout: Search Console and Bing Webmaster, which remain unconfigured and are the single largest missing input to all of this. Every judgement in this document rests on sampled SERPs rather than on what Foreland actually receives impressions for, and that is a weaker basis than it needs to be.

## What would change this plan

If Search Console shows Foreland already receives meaningful impressions for terms this document treats as unwon, the sequence changes and the emphasis moves to click-through rather than position. If the publication's second edition draws a citation from Knight Frank or a Tier 3 law firm, the off-site constraint loosens a year early and the content programme can slow. And if a Tier 1 competitor starts publishing at depth, the coverage-consistency advantage narrows quickly, because none of it is proprietary.
