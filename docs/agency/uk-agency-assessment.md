# UK yacht agency: entry assessment

Prepared 21 September 2026. Internal working document, not for circulation.

## Recommendation

Enter, but as a contained pilot rather than a business line, and on terms that put no Foreland cash at risk.

Run one season in London and the Solent under an endorsed sister brand, funds in advance on every call, no float, no staff, no van, no office. Target three attended calls sourced through overseas agency networks. Judge it on the management, refit and technical support leads it produces rather than on agency fees, because the fee pool alone will not justify the effort.

The reason to do it at all is not the agency margin. It is that the agent is the first person a visiting yacht speaks to in the UK, and Foreland currently has no way of being that person.

## What the business is

Port and shore agency for visiting yachts. Berth booking, Border Force and customs clearance, pilotage ordering, bunkering, provisioning, waste, transport, security, guest logistics, and handling the disbursement account. This is the MGMT model. It is not crew placement and it is not yacht management.

## The market

Figures from Marine Industry News, 13 May 2026, reporting data presented by Tim Mayer of Group 1851:

- 289 superyachts passed through waters off southern England between January and November 2025.
- 258 of those bypassed UK marinas, berthing in France or anchoring offshore.
- Marina visits recorded: Gunwharf Quays 35, Ocean Village 31, Poole 26.
- Estimated value lost to the UK economy, 10 to 15 million pounds a year. That is total shore spend, not agency fees.

MGMT's own account of the trend, from its website: around 12 attended visits a year in 2018/19, more than 60 in 2024, around 70 expected in 2025. Treat this as MGMT's attended visits rather than a national total.

So the realistic number of agency-worthy UK calls is somewhere between 100 and 150 a year across the whole country, growing quickly in percentage terms from a very low base.

Estimated total UK agency fee pool: 300,000 to 700,000 pounds a year, split between five or six firms plus the marinas that handle it themselves. This is an estimate. No UK agent publishes fee figures, so it cannot be verified.

## The finding that changes the entry barrier

Berth access in central London is not relationship-locked.

The PLA's published rates schedule states that London Cruise and Yacht, a partnership between MBNA Thames Clippers and the Port of Tilbury, holds the PLA contract to manage the three central London moorings: Greenwich Ship Tier, Tower Bridge Upper and George Stairs Tier. All associated services, mooring and unmooring, refuse, black water, grey water and fresh water, are booked through them on a minimum of 48 hours notice.

Pilotage is a provisional order to London Port Control at Gravesend and is compulsory over 40m. Tower Bridge lifts require 24 hours notice by email, carry no charge, and the request must come from the ship's agent, owner or master.

The London arrival chain is therefore four bookings that any competent operator can make. What the incumbents hold is not access. It is knowing what to book and in what order, and answering the phone at two in the morning when the pilot slips.

## How the money works

Shipping convention is an agency fee of roughly 5 to 15 per cent of the disbursement account, with the agent advancing supplier costs. MGMT has moved off that model and publishes a flat first-booking fee, then an hourly rate, capped at a daily rate above six hours and a weekly rate above four days. That tells you the percentage model does not generate enough on UK volumes.

### Worked example: 55m yacht, five nights in the Upper Pool, arriving from the Channel

Using PLA 2025 published rates:

- Pilotage inbound, NE Spit to Gravesend 698 pounds, Gravesend to Sector D 768 pounds, plus a 5 per cent PNPF levy and a 386 pound boarding charge. Approximately 1,925 pounds.
- Pilotage outbound, similar. Round trip approximately 3,800 to 4,000 pounds.
- PLA conservancy estuary charge at 0.019 pounds per GT. Around 13 pounds on a vessel of this size and therefore irrelevant.
- Mooring at Tower Bridge Upper, price on application through London Cruise and Yacht.
- Tower Bridge lift, no charge.
- Fuel, waste, provisioning, cars, security on top.

Total disbursement account, roughly 15,000 to 40,000 pounds for the week. The agency fee is the smallest line on it, perhaps 1,500 to 4,000 pounds. That is the number the business lives on.

## What entry requires

None of it is capital-intensive. In order:

Insurance. ITIC is the ship agents' insurer and has covered agents' disbursement recovery since 1925. Ask them to extend policy B038 to yacht agency work. This is a phone call, not a new placement.

The cash rule, which is the condition of entry rather than a preference. Pro-forma disbursement account issued before arrival, cleared funds received in advance, no supplier payment advanced from Foreland's own money. With no facility since the loan refusal in July 2026, there is no alternative.

VAT treatment. Confirm with the accountant before the first call that genuine agency disbursements sit outside the scope of VAT under HMRC's conditions, and that the agency fee is billed separately. Get this right at the start, not after the first invoice.

The authority and supplier map, at Appendix A. A day to build, a season to make real.

AYSS membership. Minimum two years trading, three sets of references from captains, from the industry and from existing member agents, refreshed every five years. Foreland reaches the two year mark shortly. With 61 members across 45 countries, being the UK name on that map is worth more than it sounds.

## Where the work comes from

In order of yield.

First, act as UK correspondent for the agency networks with no UK office. BWA's Mediterranean and Scandinavian offices have clients heading north every summer and nobody to hand them to. The same is true across much of the AYSS membership. This is inbound work from professional counterparties who understand pro-forma accounts and pay in advance, which answers the cash problem and the marketing problem at the same time.

Second, management companies and owner's representatives with a yacht coming to the UK.

Third, and slowest, captains direct, worked around the events calendar: Cowes Week, the Fastnet, Wimbledon, Ascot, the northern summer transits to Scotland and Norway, and the autumn run south.

## Risks

The fee pool is small and will not replace a management retainer.

The work is reactive, seasonal and out of hours, and it collides directly with refit and owner's representation travel. You cannot be in Gdynia and meet a yacht at Tower Bridge.

Reputational exposure runs the wrong way. A missed pilot order at 0200 damages the owner's representation brand, which is worth considerably more than the agency fee.

Posture. Agency puts Foreland in a supplier role to captains, on the other side of the table from where owner's representation sits. This is the reason for a separate brand and for the at-cost, no-rebate commitment. Handled openly it extends the independence position. Handled casually it undermines it.

## The pilot and its decision test

One season. London and the Solent only. Funds in advance. No float, no staff.

Target three attended calls, sourced through overseas agency networks rather than cold marketing.

Decision at the end of the season: if the pilot has not produced at least one qualified lead for management, technical support or refit, close it and keep the AYSS listing dormant. Agency fees alone are not a reason to continue.

### Regulatory points worth holding accurately

Temporary Admission allows a qualifying non-UK yacht up to 18 months in UK waters without triggering import VAT, subject to conditions and private use.

UK ETA applies from 2026 to owners, guests and non-exempt crew arriving on their own vessel. Professional crew joining a yacht that will leave UK waters, and who are not taking UK employment ashore, are exempt under the Home Office Seafarer Transit Scheme. Confirm the current position before advising any individual.

Fuel. In Great Britain private pleasure craft may still take red diesel and pay the duty difference on the propulsion proportion, commonly handled on a 60/40 declaration. The propulsion ban applies in Northern Ireland from 1 October 2021. HMRC has been carrying out marina inspections. Do not repeat the common assumption that red diesel is banned throughout the UK, because in Great Britain it is not.
